### On marathon pace

You're right that I read this too conservatively. Correcting for bike load moves it a full 10 sec/km.

**Almost none of your running has been done fresh.** Of 164 runs over the last 16 months, only 17 had no bike the same day and no significant ride the day before — and even those carried a median ATL of 104. Every fast run in your file was set under Ironman load.

The clearest example is your half PR:

| 2025-05-18 · 21.2 km @ **3:45/km**, HR 158 |
|---|
| 1.4 h bike **that same day** · 2.3 h bike the day before · ATL **137** |

A 1:19 half off a brick, mid-Ironman-block, at HR 158. That is not a rested half marathon.

The same pattern runs through everything:

- **10K at 3:57, HR 170** (Apr 4) — maximal effort, but the day after a 1.7 h ride, at ATL 135
- **24.1 km at 4:17, HR only 141** (Aug 2025) — an aerobic long run, carrying ATL 148
- **Rockford, 4:08 normalized at HR 148** — off a 90 km bike, and HR 148 is well below the 158–170 you hit when actually working

That last one matters most. You rode Rockford conservatively at IF 0.80, ran the half at HR 148, and described yourself as flat. There was room left.

**So what does that make marathon pace?** A fresh, tapered open half for you is somewhere around 3:50–3:58. Marathon pace normally sits 15–20 sec/km off half pace, which lands at **4:10–4:18** on a flat course.

I've set the band at **4:15–4:25** rather than the raw calculation, for three reasons: Yonkers is hilly, you'll have four marathon-specific long runs rather than a full block, and the honest counterweight in the data is **Aug 9 — 21.2 km at 4:35, HR 146, at ATL 70**. That's your current run fitness, three weeks off a DNF, and it's slower than where you were in Aug 2025. Five weeks of focused running with the bike load down will move it a lot, but it hasn't moved yet.

That band is **2:59–3:07**.

**A note on your 2000m sessions**: they average ~4:27 including warm-up and recovery, which reads far slower than your threshold really is. The library note on those says "don't exceed prescribed pace" — they're capped Ironman work, deliberately submaximal. The threshold sessions below are run at genuine L2.

## The back

You wrote that it needs real attention and that more training won't fix it. That's still true — this plan works around it, it doesn't solve it. Worth a professional look regardless of how the next six weeks go.

**Two lifts a week, every week.** Posterior chain and anti-extension core — what holds position when you're tired at 32 km. Keep heavy spinal loading off the day before a long run.

**All bike work is flat and RPM-free.** This matters more now that bike volume is up. Skip these from your library for the next six weeks:

- *Specific Strength and Zone 3* — 7×2:30 climbing at ≤50 rpm
- *Z2 with Z3 Big Gear* — ≤55 rpm torque blocks
- *Mixed Terrain* — climbs at Z4 low RPM
- *Z4 Big Gear + Z5 RPM Free* — the big-gear half

Low-cadence climbing is the exact load pattern you identified as the problem at Lake Placid, and it's the least useful bike work for a marathon. Easy call.

**Watch the downhills.** If Yonkers is as rolling as its reputation, descending is what will find your back late. Run some downhill in the Aug 23 and Aug 30 long runs so it isn't new on race day — but don't go hunting for it.

---

## Fueling

You started Lake Placid with a GI issue you already knew about. Six weeks is enough to actually solve this rather than hope.

Four long runs — Aug 16, Aug 23, Aug 30, Sep 6 — are the test sessions:

1. **Same products every time.** Whatever you'll use on Sep 20, use it in all four. Nothing new after Aug 30.
2. **Start at 60 g carbs/hour**, build toward 90 if it sits well. A 3+ hour race needs real intake.
3. **Same breakfast, same timing** before Aug 30 and Sep 6.
4. **Write down what happens.** If something goes wrong twice, it's the product, not the day.

If the gut is still a problem after the Aug 30 rehearsal, that's a strong signal to see someone — you'd have three weeks, which is enough to act on.

## What I'd change

**If the back flares**: drop to 4 runs/week, keep the Tuesday intervals and the long run, move lost volume to the bike. The long run is the one session that can't be replaced.

**If you decide not to race it**: keep everything through Aug 30, then hold ~50 km/week instead of tapering and treat Sep 20 as a long training day at 4:40/km.

**If the Aug 30 long run goes badly**: the plan doesn't change, the target does. Six weeks isn't long enough to fix fitness, but it's plenty long enough to pick the right pace.
