March was built around a four-week Z3/Z4 intensity block that started at the end of February: structured bike intervals, progressive runs, and long weekend rides, with swim frequency held steady. By the end of the month I was absorbing serious load and CTL kept climbing.

There was also a half marathon in the mix. "Meia Chegando" (half marathon coming) showed up as a session tag mid-month, pointing to a race or time trial that added intensity to an already hard block. And March ended with max-effort swim tests at 200m and 400m to get some baseline numbers.

## Swim

The swim stayed aerobic while the bike and run carried the intensity, which is the right periodization. Stacking Z3/Z4 swim work on top of a hard bike/run phase adds fatigue without much return.

- **A1 and A2 maintenance sets**: most swims stayed aerobic, steady water time without digging a hole
- **Lap swimming** (mid-week): volume to keep feel and technique sharp
- **Progressivo A1→A2→A3**: progressive sets through the zones to sharpen pace sense
- **Sessão Equipamento**: paddles and pull buoy for upper-body strength and stroke mechanics
- **Max effort tests** at month end: 1×200m and 1×400m all-out, the first benchmarks of the year

The tests matter. My 200m and 400m times calibrate training zones and show where I stand against race-pace targets. Doing them at the end of a hard month rather than fresh makes them conservative.

---

## Bike

The bike is where the Z3/Z4 block lived:

- **BLOCO Z3>Z4 (semanas 2, 3, 4 de 4)**: weekly interval sessions that got longer and harder each week
- **Long weekend rides** for volume: 161 km on Mar 7, 162 km on Mar 14, 129 km on Mar 22, building endurance under the intervals
- **Força Específica e Zona 3**: strength-endurance work, holding Zone 3 for long stretches instead of cruising
- **Bricks**: Mar 28 paired a 130 km Z3 bike with a run straight off it, the most race-specific session of the month
- **Big gear subidas** (weeks 2, 3, 4): February's hill-strength work continued with longer reps (7×35s → 7×40s → 7×50s)

Going from 6×30s in February to 7×50s by week 4 of March is a real increase in muscular load. Lake Placid demands it.

---

## Run

Running told the clearest story of the month:

- **Long aerobic runs** up to 22–23 km: the Mar 8 "Longo em Pace Fácil" and Mar 15 "Rodando em Ritmo Super Tranquilo" were true long runs, steady Z1/Z2 time on feet
- **Weekly interval progression**: each week's quality run pushed a bit harder, from Z2/Z3 mixes to full Z4 efforts by week 4
- **Meia Chegando** (half marathon coming) on Mar 11: likely a race or prep session that added a hard effort to a loaded week
- **Brick runs**: 7 km off the 130 km ride on Mar 28. These sessions train running on tired legs, which is what Ironman comes down to

Volume went up from February and I handled it. That's the signal you want mid-build: can you do more without breaking?

---

## Strength

Six strength sessions, the most in a month so far. As total load went up, strength work kept pace instead of getting dropped.

Keeping strength going through a hard bike/run block takes discipline. It's also what kept injury risk in check as run volume climbed.

---

## Fitness Metrics at End of Month

Going from CTL 103 to 119 in a month while keeping TSB near zero (-11.5) is a good result. I'm absorbing load well. At CTL 119, Ironman performance starts to feel within reach.

Since ATL and CTL ended nearly equal, March didn't leave a big fatigue debt. April can build on it without a long recovery first.

---

## What's Next

April opens with an FTP test, the first structured power benchmark of the year. That result resets all my bike zones for the final build to Lake Placid. April is also where training shifts from general base and threshold work to more race-specific intensity. **The fitness is there. April is about sharpening it.**
