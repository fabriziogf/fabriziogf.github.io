## Swim

Eight sessions, 6 hours, 20.6 km — all of them before the race.

The work was sharp and short: 2×10×100m (A3 and A1→A2) on Jul 1, 12×150m at varying intensity on Jul 3, a progressive A1→A2→A3 on Jul 7, a lipolytic 3.7 km on Jul 8. Then a swim at the race venue on Jul 15 and a short lap swim on Jul 17 to keep the feel.

Race day: 59:16. Sub-hour for the first time. The race swim didn't sync as its own file, so it isn't in the numbers above.

**And I haven't been in the water since.** Twelve days. That's the clearest thing this month's data says about where my head is.

---

## Bike

Fifteen sessions, 28.6 hours, 971 km. Still the dominant discipline, but it splits cleanly into three parts.

**Before the race** (Jul 2–17): nine rides. Low-cadence work on Jul 2, a Z3 tempo of 86 km on Jul 5, 104 km of easy miles on Jul 11, big gear into Z4 on Jul 12. Then it drops — 39 km of "very, very easy" on Jul 16 and the structured pre-race spin on Jul 17.

**The race** (Jul 19): 178 km, 5:04:11, 285 TSS, IF 0.75. My best time on that course, on a windy day, with 2,500m of climbing.

**After** (Jul 23–31): five rides, 216 km. No structure and no targets. Every one of them is just called "Road Cycling" in the file — 30 to 55 km at whatever pace felt right that morning.

That last group is the honest picture. I still want to ride. I just don't have anything to train for.

---

## Run

Thirteen sessions, 11.8 hours, 145 km.

Pre-race running was short and specific: 5×1000m on Jul 10, a 15 km Z2→Z3 tempo on Jul 5, brick runs off the bike on Jul 8 and Jul 12. Nothing long — the long runs were done in June and there was no reason to add fatigue.

Race day: 23.7 km before I stopped. It's the only run in the file that ends with a decision instead of a finish line.

Post-race: four jogs between 5 and 10.5 km, starting six days after the race. My back needed that time. The legs came back faster than I expected.

## A Caveat on Run TSS

One thing I have to flag, because it changes how to read every run number above.

TrainingPeaks seems to have changed how it scores running TSS in mid-June. Same runs, lower numbers. I noticed it pulling this month's data and went looking for a clean comparison. This is the one I found:

| | May 29 | Jun 24 |
|---|---|---|
| Distance | 11.16 km | 11.16 km |
| Time | 55:01 | 55:01 |
| Normalized pace | 4:56/km | 4:56/km |
| Avg HR | 127 | 124 |
| **TSS** | **100.5** | **55.1** |

Same route, same pace, durations a third of a second apart, 26 days apart on the calendar. The second one scored 45% lower.

It isn't just those two. For the previous eighteen months my runs came back between 100 and 155 TSS per hour. From June 17 onward every single one lands between 55 and 76. The last run scored on the old scale was Rockford on June 14.

**I think the new numbers are the correct ones.** On the old scale, easy Z2 long runs were coming back with an IF above 1.0 — that would mean holding above threshold for two hours, which never happened. The old scores were inflated. But run TSS before and after mid-June are not the same unit, and anything that sums them is mixing two scales.

Three sessions, the last one on July 9. Nothing since.

Same pattern as the swim. Strength is the first thing I drop when there's no race on the calendar, and it's usually the thing I regret dropping.

## Fitness Metrics at End of Month

CTL fell from 131.6 to 103.6. A 28-point drop in a month.

| Month end | CTL |
|-----------|-----|
| January | 79.2 |
| February | 103.0 |
| March | 118.8 |
| April | 133.6 |
| May | 140.5 |
| June | 131.6 |
| July | 103.6 |

That's the steepest drop of the year. Two weeks of taper and then a week and a half of easy riding will do that. TSB is +51.4, which is far higher than useful — it means fresh, but it also means undertrained compared to where I was.

**Part of that 28-point drop is the TSS change, not lost fitness.** Running was 30% of July's TSS and all of it is scored on the new scale, while June's CTL was built mostly on the old one. Scored the old way, July's running would have come in at roughly double — something like 800 more TSS on the month. The fitness loss is real. It's just smaller than 28 points of CTL makes it look, and I won't have a clean year-over-year comparison again until twelve months from now.

The peak was May at 140.5. I'm now roughly back where I was in February.

**Some of this is fine and some of it isn't.** Coming down after an Ironman is correct and necessary. Coming down with nothing on the other side is how you end up rebuilding from scratch in six months.

## What's Next

Will fly to Kona in October to watch my father.

Not sure what my training will look like for the rest of the year. Ironman training gave me a structure and without a race every session becomes optional. Two things I want to sort out before committing to anything:

**The back.** Was not a fitness problem and it needs real attention. Years of rowing and two bike accidents the last two years were surely contributors.

**The stomach.** I started the race with a GI issue I already knew about. Whatever the next target is, I'd rather understand that than hope it doesn't come back.

For August the plan is simple: keep riding, get back in the pool, restart strength. Don't want to lose the base.
