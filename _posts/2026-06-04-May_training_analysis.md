That's the headline.

## Swim

Twelve swims, steady every week, mostly at A1/A2 aerobic effort. That's right when the bike and run carry the heavy load.

Key sessions:
- **A1 and A1>A2 sets**: the backbone, aerobic technique work at controlled intensity
- **12×150m varying intensity**: speed and aerobic effort in the same set, practicing shifting gears mid-swim
- **Lipolítico and Melhor Média + A2**: fat-metabolism and pace-quality sets for efficiency at low to moderate effort
- **Progressivo A1→A2→A3**: building through the zones to sharpen the feel of accelerating
- **Lap Swimming**: plain aerobic volume

Swimming is still the sport I feel least confident in. But 12 sessions with no missed weeks, all in the prescribed zones, builds the background fitness that shows up on race day, even if no single session feels dramatic.

---

## Bike

Cycling carried most of the load, which is where it belongs in Ironman prep. The week alternated between:

- **Long Z2 rides** (3–5 hours): May 9 (120 km), May 16 (183 km), May 25 (104 km), May 30 (160 km). The foundation: aerobic base, fat adaptation, time in the saddle
- **L2/Z4 intervals**: weekly structured sets, usually 7 × 7'30" at Z4 with short recoveries. This is where fitness actually moves
- **Bricks**: several rides ended with a run off the bike, training the body to switch fuel and gait under fatigue

The 5-hour rides feel almost meditative when they go well and break you down when they don't. This month they went well.

Total cycling volume: about 1,600 km over the block.

---

## Run

This is where it gets interesting.

In the past, raising run volume and intensity together has been my formula for injury: tight calves, IT band, the usual. This block I turned up both, harder intervals *and* more weekly kilometers, and stayed healthy. The difference wasn't fitness. It was discipline.

I ran every session at the prescribed effort. Easy runs were actually easy. Z2 runs didn't drift into Z3. Intervals were hard, but they stopped when they were supposed to. That's boring to execute and hard to stick to when you feel good and want to push. **But it's the only thing that makes the volume sustainable.**

Key run sessions:
- **Ironman> 5×2000m Z3>4** (three times in the block): the defining session of the month. 10 km of hard intervals at race-specific effort, in three separate weeks
- **Ironman> 3×3000m Z3 and 4×3000m Z3**: longer reps at slightly lower intensity. Nine to twelve kilometers of steady Z3 teaches you to hold pace as fatigue builds
- **Ritmo em Zona 2** (17 km): long aerobic runs for race endurance
- **2 horas bem levinho** (2 hours easy, ~24 km): true long runs at low intensity
- **Corridinha Fácil**: recovery runs that protected the hard days
- **Brick runs** after long rides: the most specific Ironman prep there is

Total run volume: about 200 km across 19 sessions.

## Strength

Six strength sessions, roughly one every five days. Full-body circuits, injury prevention first (minibands, core activation), then compound lifts (squats, Romanian deadlifts, chest press, rows).

Strength is often the first thing dropped when volume spikes. Keeping it in, even in shorter sessions, maintains the neuromuscular base that keeps you running efficiently when tired.

---

## Fitness Metrics at End of Block

A CTL of 140 is a high level of fitness. Most competitive age-groupers race Ironman with a CTL between 80 and 120. Being at 140 with a TSB of +22 means the work is done and I'm fresh, the right combination before a tune-up race.

ATL dropping while CTL holds is what a taper should look like.

---

## Rockford 70.3 Plan

Rockford is a tune-up, not a target race. The goal is a clean race at controlled effort, not a PR at the expense of Lake Placid prep. Racing a 70.3 at 85–90% while holding form gives race-specific stress without the recovery cost of going all out.

What I'll watch: swim exit position, T1/T2 execution, bike pacing (it's easy to overcook a 70.3 on fresh legs), and running off the bike with good form.

---

## What's Next

Lake Placid is 45 days away. The next block is about sharpening: hold CTL close to where it is, let ATL keep falling, and add some race-specific intensity without digging a new hole. A full Ironman taper usually starts 2–3 weeks out, which puts the last big week around June 28.

**Going into the final phase healthy, fit, and without injury baggage is more than I could say after some past blocks.** That's the win from May.
