---
title: "January 2026 Training Analysis"
---

This is the first in a series of monthly training posts. Each one looks back at what actually happened: the numbers, the sessions, and what they mean for the season. I'm not just logging data. I want to spot patterns, catch drift early, and hold myself to the process.

January 2026 was a base-building month. No race was close, but Ironman Lake Placid on July 19 anchors the whole year. Everything before it is groundwork.

---

## Overall Stats

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Completed sessions | 38 |
| Total training hours | ~47h |
| Total distance | ~1,204 km |
| Total TSS | 2,664 |
| End-of-month CTL | 79.2 |
| End-of-month ATL | 50.6 |
| End-of-month TSB | +32.8 |

38 sessions in 31 days is solid but not maxed out. Several days had one session, and some lighter days were planned. The load was real but controlled.

---

## Weekly Load Progression

| Week | TSS |
|------|-----|
| Dec 29–Jan 4 | 469 |
| Jan 5–11 | 705 |
| Jan 12–18 | 827 |
| Jan 19–25 | 491 |
| Jan 26–31 | 174 |

Load climbed through week 3, then dropped hard in weeks 4–5. The last week was a recovery week: only Zwift rides and a couple of easy sessions. That was on purpose. It's how you absorb a block without piling up chronic fatigue.

---

## Swim

Swim volume was light: a few technique sessions, including parachute drills and A1-paced sets. These don't build much swim fitness. They keep my feel for the water and my technique while volume is low.

Swimming has the most room for improvement of my three sports, but January wasn't the month to chase it. Building the aerobic base on the bike and run comes first.

---

## Bike

Cycling carried most of the load, as it should in a base phase:

- **Zwift Z2 rides** (Tempus Fugit in Watopia, flat and steady): aerobic work without terrain dictating effort
- **Weekend long rides**: 120 km on Jan 3, 140 km on Jan 10, 132 km on Jan 17, the backbone of the block
- **Shorter mid-week Z2 sessions**: keeping the aerobic engine turning without heavy fatigue

Intensity stayed low on purpose. In base, the goal is time at aerobic effort, not heroics.

---

## Run

Run volume was modest, which is right for this stage. The session names, "Base 2026" and "Intensidade bem Fácil, FC baixa" (very easy, low heart rate), say it all: aerobic conditioning, not development.

A 14 km run on Jan 11 at a comfortable pace and a couple of shorter mid-week runs. No intervals, no tempo. The goal was frequency and easy miles to rebuild the base that Ironman run prep will need later.

---

## Strength

Two strength sessions: "Treino A - BASE" and "Treino B - BASE." Maintenance volume, not development. This early in the year, showing up is the goal. Building durability now makes the harder months safer.

---

## Fitness Metrics at End of Month

CTL (Chronic Training Load, roughly my fitness) ended January at 79. That's a reasonable starting point for an Ironman in July. The high TSB (Training Stress Balance, or freshness) of +32.8 comes from ending on a recovery week. I'm fresh, and the fitness from weeks 2–3 is being absorbed.

CTL builds from here.

---

## What's Next

February is where base building starts in earnest. The plan: more weekly hours, mostly aerobic intensity, and a steady multi-sport routine that carries through spring. **CTL needs to reach the 120–140 range by May to be on track for Lake Placid.**
