August was the first month of the Yonkers build. I'm running the Yonkers Marathon on September 20, and I wrote about the [training plan](/Yonkers_training_plan/) in the middle of the month. My coach adjusted it a little along the way.

The end of the month didn't go as planned. On August 29 we drove 12 hours from Chicago to New Jersey, on our way to New York for a new job. That drive landed on the most important weekend of the block.

Running went up a lot. Riding, swimming and strength went down. Most of the key work still got done.

## The Plan and What Changed

The basic structure stayed the same: one hard run a week, one long run, easy runs in between, and riding on the other days. My coach changed a few things:

- The hard runs moved mostly to Wednesday and became longer repeats, 5×2 km and then 7×2 km, with walking breaks.
- Race pace work moved inside the long run. Instead of one block at the end, it became two sets of 3 km repeats with easy running between them.
- The 30 km long run was completely easy, because it came two days after intervals.

The calendar also got in the way. I wasn't available on the weekend of August 22–23, so the 30 km run moved to Friday. The next weekend was the drive.

## Swim

Three sessions, 1.8 hours, 5.8 km. Easy lap swims on August 2, 10 and 17.

The plan was one swim a week. I did that for the first half of the month and then stopped. There was nothing after August 17, when the move started taking up most of my time.

---

## Bike

Ten rides, 16.3 hours, 525 km.

The plan asked for 3.5 to 4 hours a week through early September. I rode about 3 hours most weeks, and almost 5 hours in the week of August 10.

The biggest ride was on August 8, before the plan started: 102 km with a Zone 3 tempo effort. After that everything was easy, mostly 40 to 60 km. There was also a 54 km ride on Zwift on August 16, and an easy 48 km two days after the drive.

Riding was the part that gave way as running went up.

---

## Run

Eighteen runs, 20 hours, 252 km. In July it was 145 km.

Five sessions tell most of the story.

**August 15, 25.7 km.** Most of the first 18 km was between 4:20 and 4:34 per km. Then I ran 5 km at 4:08–4:11 before an easy finish. Heart rate reached 179 in that fast block.

**August 19, 5×2 km.** The workout said "slightly tiring to tiring." I ran the repeats at 3:40–3:47 per km, and heart rate reached 172–175 by the end of each one. That's faster than it was written.

**August 21, 30.5 km.** All easy. Every kilometer was between 4:40 and 5:00, with heart rate mostly in the 130s and low 140s. It was the longest run of the block and very even.

**August 25, 7×2 km.** 14 km of repeats at 3:39–3:44 per km, with two-minute walks between them. Heart rate was in the 170s by the end of most repeats. I ran short on carbs near the end. I took one gel before starting and another after the third repeat, and I should have taken a third. That's the kind of thing these sessions are supposed to catch.

**August 30, 27.7 km, the day after the drive.** This was supposed to be the key session of the plan. It was written as 2×3 km at tempo, 10 km easy, then 2×3 km at race pace.

The first two repeats went well, at 3:57–4:01 per km. After that it fell apart. On the "easy" 10 km my heart rate stayed between 154 and 172, even at 4:30–4:55 pace. The second set of repeats didn't happen. I ran one fast kilometer, slowed down, and jogged the last 6 km at 5:20–5:50.

I can't separate the drive from everything else going on that weekend, but 12 hours in a car the day before is the obvious difference. The run was supposed to help settle my race pace, and it didn't.

## Strength

Five sessions, 4.2 hours, on August 2, 5, 10, 13 and 25.

That's about twice a week for the first half of the month, then one session in the last two weeks. Same pattern as the swim. Strength is the thing my back needs most, so it's the part I most want to get back to.

Second, running TSS is still on the lower scale that started in June, which I wrote about in the [July analysis](/July_training_analysis/). A month with a lot of running scores lower than the Ironman months did, so these numbers aren't directly comparable to the first half of the year.

## What's Next

Yonkers is on Sunday, September 20.

Since the move, training has settled into the same pattern: intervals on Wednesday, a long easy ride on Saturday, and the long run with race pace blocks on Sunday. The tempo blocks in the September long runs have mostly been between 3:50 and 4:07 per km.

The plan's race pace is 4:15–4:25 per km. The August 30 run was supposed to settle that and didn't. The 2 km repeats in August and the September tempo blocks are why I still think that range is conservative. I'll settle on a number before Sunday.

Swimming and strength haven't come back yet since the move. For race week, the goal is simple: short easy runs, rest, and get the fueling right.
