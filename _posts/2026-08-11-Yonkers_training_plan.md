title: "Yonkers Training Plan"
---

I'm running the Yonkers Marathon on September 20. That's five and a half weeks away.

After the Lake Placid DNF I had nothing on the calendar. The [July numbers](/July_training_analysis/) showed what that looks like — I kept riding, and the swimming and strength work stopped. A marathon in September gives me something to train for. It's close enough that I don't have to rebuild anything, and it's different enough that I'm not doing Ironman training without an Ironman.

I wrote out a plan. [The full version is here](/yonkers-plan/), with every session, both weekend options, and pace targets. This post is the summary.

---

## Working out the pace

The schedule was the easy part. Figuring out how fast I can run was harder.

The first version came out too slow. The reason is that almost none of my running has been done fresh. I looked at 164 runs over the last sixteen months. Only 17 had no bike on the same day and no long ride the day before. Even those were run with an average ATL of 104.

My half marathon PR is the clearest case. 21.2 km at 3:45/km. I rode 1.4 hours that morning and 2.3 hours the day before, at ATL 137. So that was a 1:19 half off a brick, in the middle of an Ironman block.

The rest of the data looks the same. The 10K at 3:57 was the day after a ride. The 24 km at 4:17 was at heart rate 141, carrying ATL 148. Rockford was off a 90 km bike at heart rate 148, and I go to 158 or higher when I'm working hard.

Adjusted for all that, the plan sets marathon pace at 4:15 to 4:25/km. That's about three hours.

---

## I think that's conservative

The plan uses 4:15 to 4:25, but I expect to run faster than that.

The bike load point goes further than the numbers above. For the last three years every run I've done has been part of an Ironman block. Off the bike, the day after a long ride, or with an ATL over 120. I've never trained running as the main thing, and there isn't a single hard run in my file done rested. So there's no data point that shows what I can actually do.

The half PR is the one I keep coming back to. 1:19 off a brick at heart rate 158. Rested and standalone, that should be well under 1:19, and marathon pace follows from half pace.

The argument on the other side is the August 9 run: 21 km at 4:35, heart rate 146, rested. That's where I am today, and it's slower than a year ago. So the plan is set at a pace I'm confident I can hold rather than the one I think I'm capable of.

I'd rather find out on August 30 than guess now. If the 16 km at race pace that day feels controlled, I'll move the target down.

## The weeks

**Week 1 (Aug 12–16)** — Get back to running at marathon pace. One threshold session and a 24 km long run with 6 km at race pace at the end.

**Week 2 (Aug 17–23)** — First full week. 5×2000m at threshold. Long run out to 28 km with 8 km at marathon pace.

**Week 3 (Aug 24–30)** — Peak week. 6×1200m intervals midweek, then 32 km on the weekend with 16 km at race pace. This is the most important week in the plan.

**Week 4 (Aug 31–Sep 6)** — Last hard week. 8×1000m, then 26 km as a full rehearsal. Race kit, race shoes, race breakfast, race fueling.

**Week 5 (Sep 7–13)** — Taper starts. Volume comes down about a third. I keep some intensity so the legs don't go flat.

**Week 6 (Sep 14–20)** — Race week. Short and easy, with 3 km at marathon pace on Thursday. Race on Sunday.

Peak volume is 67 km in a week, up from about 50 now. Every week has two versions, with the long run on either Saturday or Sunday, because I don't always know in advance which one the weekend allows.

---

## The other sports

I'm not dropping the bike. Three and a half to four hours a week through September 6, all of it flat and easy. Partly because I like riding, and partly because it adds aerobic work that would otherwise have to come from more running.

One swim a week. Two lifts a week, every week.

The lifting isn't optional. It's the thing I stopped after Lake Placid and the thing my back needs most.

---

## The lifting

Two sessions a week, and this is the part I'm most careful about.

I'm following a six-week back, hip and core program my physio put together after Lake Placid. It has three phases and a ten-minute daily routine that runs underneath all of them. I wrote a [lifting plan](/yonkers-lifting/) that sequences that program against the running and adds a few things a marathon needs.

Two things came out of putting them side by side.

The first is a timing problem. The heaviest phase of the PT program starts on August 30, which is the same day as the 32 km long run, in the biggest running week I have. I moved that phase to September 1 so the two don't collide.

The second is a gap. The PT program has no calf work at all. That's correct for back rehab, but the soleus takes six to eight times bodyweight per stride when running, and it's the first thing to complain when weekly volume jumps by a third. Mine is about to go from 50 to 67 km. So I added calf and soleus work to both sessions, slower eccentrics on the step-downs for the downhills at Yonkers, and a small amount of ankle stiffness work that comes out during the taper.

The daily ten-minute routine is the part I won't skip. If a week goes badly, I'll drop a strength session before I drop that.

---

## The back and the stomach

All the bike work in this plan is flat, with free cadence. I took the low cadence and big gear sessions out for six weeks. That's the load that gave me trouble at Lake Placid, and it isn't especially useful for a marathon. Yonkers is hilly, so the downhills are what will bother my back late in the race. I'll run some downhill in the long runs so it isn't new on race day.

For the stomach, the four long runs between now and September 6 are test sessions. Same products, same breakfast, same timing, every time. Nothing new after August 30. If it's still a problem after the rehearsal, I'll have three weeks to go see someone about it.

Neither of these is a fitness problem. That was the lesson from July and it hasn't changed.

---

## What I don't know yet

Whether 4:15 is right, or 4:25, or whether both are slow.

The August 30 long run will tell me more than any of this analysis. Six weeks isn't enough time to change my fitness much, but it's enough to work out what pace to run.
