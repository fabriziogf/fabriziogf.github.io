title: "Yonkers Training Plan"
---

I'm running the Yonkers Marathon on September 20, five and a half weeks from now.

After the Lake Placid DNF I had nothing on the calendar. The [July numbers](/July_training_analysis/) showed what that looks like: I kept riding, and the swimming and strength work stopped. A September marathon gives me something to train for. It's close enough that I don't have to rebuild anything, and different enough that I'm not doing Ironman training without an Ironman.

I wrote out a plan. [The full version is here](/yonkers-plan/), with every session, both weekend options, and pace targets. This post is the summary.

---

## Working out the pace

The schedule was the easy part. Figuring out how fast I can run was harder.

The first version came out too slow, because almost none of my running has been done fresh. I looked at 164 runs over the last sixteen months. Only 17 had no bike on the same day and no long ride the day before. Even those averaged an ATL of 104.

My half marathon PR is the clearest example: 21.2 km at 3:45/km. I rode 1.4 hours that morning and 2.3 hours the day before, at ATL 137. So that was a 1:19 half off a brick, in the middle of an Ironman block.

The rest of the data looks the same. The 10K at 3:57 was the day after a ride. The 24 km at 4:17 was at heart rate 141, carrying ATL 148. Rockford was off a 90 km bike at heart rate 148, and I go to 158 or higher when I'm working hard.

Adjusting for all that, the plan sets marathon pace at 4:15 to 4:25/km. That's about three hours.

---

## Why I think that's conservative

The plan uses 4:15 to 4:25, but I expect to run faster.

The bike-load point goes beyond the numbers above. For three years every run I've done has been part of an Ironman block: off the bike, the day after a long ride, or with ATL over 120. I've never trained running as the main thing, and there isn't a single hard run in my file done rested. So no data point shows what I can actually do.

The half PR is the one I keep coming back to. 1:19 off a brick at heart rate 158. Rested and standalone, that should be well under 1:19, and marathon pace follows from half pace.

The case on the other side is the August 9 run: 21 km at 4:35, heart rate 146, rested. That's where I am today, and it's slower than a year ago. So the plan uses a pace I'm confident I can hold, not the one I think I'm capable of.

I'd rather find out on August 30 than guess now. **If the 16 km at race pace that day feels controlled, I'll move the target down.**

## The weeks

**Week 1 (Aug 12–16)**: get back to running at marathon pace. One threshold session and a 24 km long run with the last 6 km at race pace.

**Week 2 (Aug 17–23)**: first full week. 5×2000m at threshold. Long run out to 28 km with 8 km at marathon pace.

**Week 3 (Aug 24–30)**: peak week. 6×1200m midweek, then 32 km on the weekend with 16 km at race pace. The most important week in the plan.

**Week 4 (Aug 31–Sep 6)**: last hard week. 8×1000m, then 26 km as a full rehearsal: race kit, race shoes, race breakfast, race fueling.

**Week 5 (Sep 7–13)**: taper starts. Volume drops about a third. I keep some intensity so the legs don't go flat.

**Week 6 (Sep 14–20)**: race week. Short and easy, with 3 km at marathon pace on Thursday. Race on Sunday.

Peak volume is 67 km a week, up from about 50 now. Every week has two versions, with the long run on Saturday or Sunday, because I don't always know which day the weekend allows.

---

## The other sports

I'm not dropping the bike: three and a half to four hours a week through September 6, all flat and easy. Partly because I like riding, and partly because it adds aerobic work that would otherwise have to come from more running.

One swim a week. Two lifts a week, every week.

The lifting isn't optional. It's what I stopped after Lake Placid, and it's what my back needs most.

---

## The lifting

Two sessions a week, and this is the part I'm most careful about.

I'm following a six-week back, hip, and core program my physio put together after Lake Placid. It has three phases and a ten-minute daily routine underneath all of them. I wrote a [lifting plan](/yonkers-lifting/) that sequences that program around the running and adds a few things a marathon needs.

Putting them side by side surfaced two things.

The first is timing. The heaviest phase of the PT program starts on August 30, the same day as the 32 km long run in my biggest running week. I moved that phase to September 1 so they don't collide.

The second is a gap. The PT program has no calf work. That's correct for back rehab, but the soleus takes six to eight times bodyweight per stride when running, and it's the first thing to complain when weekly volume jumps by a third. Mine is about to go from 50 to 67 km. So I added calf and soleus work to both sessions, slower eccentrics on the step-downs for the Yonkers downhills, and a little ankle stiffness work that drops out during the taper.

The daily ten-minute routine is what I won't skip. If a week goes badly, I'll drop a strength session before I drop that.

---

## The back and the stomach

All the bike work in this plan is flat, at whatever cadence feels natural. I took out the low-cadence and big-gear sessions for six weeks. That's the load that gave me trouble at Lake Placid, and it isn't very useful for a marathon. Yonkers is hilly, so the downhills are what will bother my back late in the race. I'll run some downhill in the long runs so it isn't new on race day.

For the stomach, the four long runs between now and September 6 are test sessions. Same products, same breakfast, same timing, every time. Nothing new after August 30. If it's still a problem after the rehearsal, I'll have three weeks to see someone about it.

**Neither of these is a fitness problem.** That was the lesson from July, and it hasn't changed.

---

## What I don't know yet

Whether 4:15 is right, or 4:25, or whether both are slow.

The August 30 long run will tell me more than any of this analysis. Six weeks isn't enough to change my fitness much, but it's enough to work out what pace to run.
