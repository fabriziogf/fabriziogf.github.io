title: "February 2026 Training Analysis"
---

February was when training stopped being maintenance and started being construction. January built the floor; February built the walls. Sessions got longer, weekly TSS climbed, and by the end of the month I was carrying real load. CTL went from 79 to 103 in four weeks. You don't get that from going through the motions.

Ironman Lake Placid is still more than five months away, but July comes fast.

---

A TSB of -30.3 means February ended fatigued. That's fine. It's what a block that actually loads the system looks like. The body adapts to stress, not comfort, and recovery will come.

## Swim

Swim quality stepped up from January. Sessions moved from pure technique to structured aerobic sets:

- **20×50m A1–A2 + 20×50m A2**: volume at controlled intensity, pushing the top of the aerobic range
- **Paraquedas + 4×400m**: parachute resistance for pulling power, then sustained pace sets
- **Natação Recuperativa**: real recovery swims that keep frequency up without adding fatigue

Pairing a harder swim mid-week with an easier one later in the week worked well. February's scheduling was more deliberate than January's.

---

## Bike

Cycling added more structure and terrain variation:

- **Long Z2 rides with RPM focus** (3-hour sessions, 120+ km): the Feb 7 and Feb 28 rides anchored weekly volume
- **Big gear climbing**: the Feb 14 session ("Volume Final com BIG GEAR subidas"). High-torque, low-cadence work builds the bike strength that turns into sustained power on long climbs
- **Start of the Z3/Z4 block**: the last days of February ("BLOCO Z3>Z4, semana 1 de 4") began a four-week intensity cycle that ran into March
- **Bricks**: Feb 21 combined a Z2/Z3 bike with a Z3 run right after, the first real brick of the year

The big gear sessions matter most. Lake Placid's bike course has long climbs, and pushing big gears without blowing up is a race-specific skill. February is the right time to start training it.

---

## Run

Run volume was clearly higher than January's, and intensity started to creep up:

- **Corrida Levinha** (easy runs, ~13 km): the bread-and-butter aerobic work
- **Progressivo Zona 2 a Zona 3**: progressive runs that start easy and finish at the top of the aerobic zone
- **Após a bike — Corrida Ritmada em Z3**: the Feb 21 brick run, at Z3. Running at race effort off the bike, even for 12 km, is real Ironman prep
- **Hills**: Feb 23 "Subidas (6×30s) + (6×40s)", short hill repeats for running strength and neuromuscular stimulus

Easy volume plus targeted strength and intensity is the right mix right now. Too much intensity too soon is how you get hurt. Too little is how you stall.

---

## Strength

Four strength sessions, up from two in January. Same structure (injury prevention, core activation, compound lifts) but more often. Steady strength work through the build is my insurance against injury when run volume jumps in spring.

---

## Fitness Metrics at End of Month

CTL going from 79 to 103 in one month is what a properly loaded block should produce. I'm going into March clearly fitter than I started February.

The -30.3 TSB is the cost. March has to manage that fatigue: absorb February's work while still building.

---

## What's Next

March finishes the four-week Z3/Z4 block that started at the end of February. Sessions get harder, with longer intervals and more specific efforts, and swim sets move toward race-relevant distances. **The question is whether I can absorb February's load and still respond to March.** That answer usually shows up in the third week.
