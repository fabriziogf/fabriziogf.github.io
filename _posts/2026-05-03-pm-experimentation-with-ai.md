---
title: "Thoughts on PMing with AI"
---

I've used AI tools for a while, at work and at home. They started as a research and writing aid and have become a central part of my day as a PM. I recently read two Anthropic posts, [one on product management in the AI era](https://claude.com/blog/product-management-on-the-ai-exponential) and [another on product development in the agentic era](https://claude.com/blog/product-development-in-the-agentic-era). They describe a framework close to the one I've slowly landed on myself.

---

The first post lays out a deliberate split: different tools for different kinds of work.

**Claude.ai is where I think.** It's my thought partner for ideas, strategic writing, and polishing stakeholder communication.

**Claude Code is where I build.** Prototypes, scripts, and quick evals. It's how I get fast feedback on product ideas.

**Cowork handles everything else.** It takes on a lot of the operational work that used to eat my week: email, to-dos, project updates and slide decks, and digging through Slack history to find out why a decision was made six months ago.

---

More broadly, the PM loop is speeding up as these tools get smarter and better at coordinating across tasks.

That happens in two ways. First, what's possible keeps changing. The first post notes that model capability improved roughly 41x on a specific task over 16 months. A constraint that made something impractical six months ago may be gone, so PMs have to keep re-checking their roadmaps.

Second, the work itself is changing. Over the past year, quick prototyping has replaced a lot of a PM's coordination work. As the second post puts it, "a spec that reads elegantly in a doc can fall apart the first time you try to build against it." Prototyping becomes part of thinking. Building a rough version surfaces problems no document review will. **The feedback is faster and more honest.**

---

I'm still figuring out what fits my workflow. But having a clear framework for using these tools on purpose has helped.
